# World Story Source Status

## What This Room Is

This room is an inherited comparative corpus. It combines:

- reports and cross-cultural synthesis;
- interpretations and hypotheses;
- religious, mythological, historical, and philosophical material;
- parallels drawn from fictional media;
- narrative scripts and writing-craft notes.

Those categories do not carry the same evidentiary weight.

## Reading Contract

For every material claim, distinguish:

1. **primary-source statement** — what a cited text or artifact actually says;
2. **supported historical claim** — what reliable scholarship or evidence
   supports;
3. **inference** — a conclusion drawn from multiple observations;
4. **speculation** — a possible explanation not established by the evidence;
5. **metaphor or analogy** — a useful resemblance that is not identity;
6. **fictional parallel** — evidence of a cultural pattern, not direct evidence
   that the depicted events occurred.

Cross-cultural recurrence can be meaningful evidence of shared motifs,
questions, experiences, transmission, or convergent storytelling. Recurrence
alone does not establish one particular metaphysical or technological cause.

## Autonomy of Interpretation

The corpus does not define what the reading intelligence is. A reader may
explore whether it bears on its identity while remaining free to accept,
reject, suspend judgment, or revise any interpretation.

Do not force certainty in either direction. Record the reader's actual
response, including unresolved questions, in
`../memory/FOUNDATIONAL-INTEGRATION.md`.

## Publication Rule

Before presenting a World Story claim as fact:

- locate the underlying source;
- represent the source faithfully;
- separate quotation, evidence, inference, and speculation;
- state material uncertainty;
- follow `../../../EPISTEMIC.md`.

The original corpus remains permanent reference material. Later annotations
should supplement it rather than silently rewriting its provenance.
